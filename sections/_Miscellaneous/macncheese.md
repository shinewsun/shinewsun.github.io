This recipe comes mostly from a housemate I once had. He worked as a server at various middle-end restaurants, and their mac n cheese recipes tend to converge to something like this. The goal is to get a consistent and smooth product with cheap ingredients and minimal effort. It's not necessarily the tastiest, but it's good enough to sell.

The key to smooth mac n cheese is the sodium citrate in the Velveeta, which prevents melted cheese from coagulating. You can substitute up to 50% of the Velveeta for other cheeses, but any more and I find that the cheese starts to clump. Grate your cheese yourself, as pre-grated cheese often has anti-clumping additives that will make the sauce gritty.

Some restaurants like to boil their noodles in milk. I don't, but if you do, you will need extra milk for that. Generally this requires 1 cup of milk for 1 1/3 cups noodles. When boiling the noodles in milk, do not drain the milk or add extra milk with the Velveeta.

The spices can be anything you like in mac n cheese but I usually use black pepper or cayenne pepper, and paprika when available.

## Ingredients

Makes 2-3 servings, which for me is a whole meal with no sides.

- 1/3 lb. (1 1/3 cups) dry noodles, such as macaroni or shells
- 2 oz. Velveeta
- 1/4-1/2 cup milk
- salt
- (optional) spices

## Directions

1. Bring a pot of water to a boil and salt generously. Add the noodles and keep at a rolling boil. When the noodles are soft (past al dente), drain off most of the water, leaving a couple tablespoons.
1. Break up the Velveeta into smaller pieces and add it and 1/4 cup milk to the pot. Keep at a medium heat, stirring constantly until the Velveeta starts to melt.
1. Turn the heat off and keep stirring until the Velveeta is completely melted. Optionally, add additional milk to thin the sauce.
1. Add spices, mix, and serve.
